# Reading track

Built from the failure modes in `ant-analogy-interstellar.md`. The analogy was good — the three
places it broke were not random, they were three distinct missing skills. This list is organised
around fixing those, not around topic.

| What broke | The skill missing | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Assumed the ant must *know* before it can *travel* | Mechanistic / evolutionary thinking — capability without understanding | 3 |
| Called a plane ride "FTL" | **Order-of-magnitude estimation.** 4.24 ly ÷ 0.1c = 42 years. One line of arithmetic kills the whole FTL claim | **0** |
| Didn't know exoplanets were found in 1992 | Check the state of the art *before* theorising | 1 |

Tier 0 is the highest-leverage thing on this page. Do it first.

---

## Tier 0 — Quantitative intuition (start here, ~1 month)

The single fix that would have caught the one real error. Every claim of the form "X seems light
years away" is a numerical claim; learn to check it in your head.

1. **Sanjoy Mahajan, *Street-Fighting Mathematics*** (MIT Press, 2010) — free legal PDF from MIT
   Press / OCW. Short. Teaches estimation, dimensional analysis, lumping, easy cases.
2. **Sanjoy Mahajan, *The Art of Insight in Science and Engineering*** (MIT Press, 2014) — also
   free. The longer, better sequel.
3. **John Harte, *Consider a Spherical Cow*** — environmental-problem estimation, worked examples.
4. **Randall Munroe, *What If?*** — the same skill, entertaining. Read it as *method*, not comedy.

**Practice, not just reading:** one Fermi problem a day for a month, written down, then checked.
Start with: how much energy to get 1 kg to 0.1c? How many stars within 20 ly? How long does a
generation ship need to be self-sufficient? If your estimate is within 10×, you've won.

## Tier 1 — Epistemology: how to know things, and how you fool yourself

5. **David Deutsch, *The Beginning of Infinity*** (2011) — **the most important book on this page
   after Tier 0.** Read it specifically as the enemy of your own analogy.
6. **Richard Feynman, "Cargo Cult Science"** (Caltech commencement, 1974) — 20 minutes, free
   online. "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself."
7. **Karl Popper, *Conjectures and Refutations*** (1963) — essays 1 and 10 suffice to start.
8. **Thomas Kuhn, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*** (1962) — read *after* Popper so you
   can see them disagree.
9. **Philip Tetlock, *Superforecasting*** (2015) — calibration; how to hold a belief at 70% rather
   than 100%. Directly relevant to "there *will* be other humans."
10. **Douglas Hubbard, *How to Measure Anything*** (2014) — practical companion to Tetlock.
11. *(Later, hard, worth it)* **E.T. Jaynes, *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science*** (2003).

## Tier 2 — Minds and their limits (your analogy's home turf)

12. **Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"** *Philosophical Review* 83 (1974) — 15 pages.
13. **Colin McGinn, "Can We Solve the Mind–Body Problem?"** *Mind* 98 (1989) — the rigorous version
    of your claim. Free PDFs online.
14. **Ed Yong, *An Immense World*** (2022) — Umwelt done empirically and beautifully. Read this
    before von Uexküll.
15. **Jakob von Uexküll, *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*** (1934) — the original.
16. **Peter Godfrey-Smith, *Other Minds*** (2016) — the octopus, i.e. a genuine alien on Earth.
17. **Daniel Dennett, *From Bacteria to Bach and Back*** (2017) — "competence without
    comprehension." The direct correction to correction #1.
18. **Douglas Hofstadter, *Gödel, Escher, Bach*** (1979) — a year-long project, not a weekend.

## Tier 3 — Why ants work: evolution, emergence, cumulative culture

19. **Hölldobler & Wilson, *Journey to the Ants*** (1994) — the accessible one. *The Ants* (1990,
    Pulitzer) is the 700-page reference if you get hooked.
20. **Deborah Gordon, *Ant Encounters*** (2010) — colony behaviour with no central plan; the
    individual ant really does know almost nothing.
21. **Joseph Henrich, *The Secret of Our Success*** (2016) — cumulative culture, the thing ants
    lack and we have.
22. **Richard Dawkins, *Climbing Mount Improbable*** (1996) — design without a designer.
23. **Gould, *Wonderful Life*** (1989) **vs. Conway Morris, *Life's Solution*** (2003) — read as a
    pair. This *is* the "will there be other humans?" debate.
24. **Cecilia Heyes, *Cognitive Gadgets*** (2018).

## Tier 4 — The cosmic question, done rigorously

25. **Milan Ćirković, *The Great Silence*** (OUP, 2018) — the scoreboard of every Fermi-paradox
    resolution. Read this before forming opinions.
26. **Lingam & Loeb, *Life in the Cosmos*** (Harvard UP, 2021) — the current rigorous textbook.
    Heavy; use as reference.
27. Papers, in this order — all short, all free:
    - Hart, *QJRAS* 16 (1975) — "they're not here, therefore they don't exist"
    - Ball, "The Zoo Hypothesis," *Icarus* 19 (1973)
    - Tipler, *QJRAS* 21 (1980) — von Neumann probes
    - Hanson, "The Great Filter" (1998, essay)
    - **Sandberg, Drexler & Ord, "Dissolving the Fermi Paradox"** — arXiv:1806.02404 (2018)
    - Kipping, *PNAS* (2020) — Bayesian abiogenesis
28. Propulsion, in this order:
    - Dyson, "Interstellar Transport," *Physics Today* (1968)
    - Forward, *J. Spacecraft and Rockets* 21(2) (1984) — laser lightsails
    - **Lubin, "A Roadmap to Interstellar Flight,"** arXiv:1604.01356 (2016)
    - Alcubierre, *Class. Quantum Grav.* 11 (1994) — then Pfenning & Ford (1997) for why not
29. **Mayor & Queloz, *Nature* 378 (1995)** — read the actual paper. Four pages. This is the moment
    we learned there was a Canada; it's worth seeing how modest it looks.

## Tier 5 — Fiction that does the philosophy properly

Not a break from the list. These are arguments in narrative form.

30. **Stanisław Lem — *His Master's Voice*** (1968), then ***Solaris*** (1961), then ***Fiasco***
    (1986). Lem is the writer closest to your instinct. Start with *His Master's Voice*.
31. **Liu Cixin, *The Three-Body Problem*** trilogy — the ant-on-the-gravestone image, the dark
    forest, dimensional collapse.
32. **Peter Watts, *Blindsight*** (2006) — intelligence without consciousness. Free online, legally.
33. **Olaf Stapledon, *Star Maker*** (1937) — still the most ambitious scale ever attempted.
34. **Ted Chiang, *Stories of Your Life and Others*** — "Story of Your Life," "Tower of Babylon."
35. **Vernor Vinge, *A Fire Upon the Deep*** (1992) — a physics that limits where thought is
    possible.
36. **Borges — "The Library of Babel," "Funes the Memorious," "On Exactitude in Science."**
37. **Edwin Abbott Abbott, *Flatland*** (1884) — the dimensional version, 100 pages, free.

## Tier 6 — If the research ambition is serious

38. **Richard Hamming, "You and Your Research"** (Bell Labs, 1986) — transcript free online. Read
    it once a year. Non-negotiable.
39. **Larry McEnerney, "The Craft of Writing Effectively"** (UChicago, free on YouTube, 80 min) —
    the best single thing on writing that gets *read*. Will change how you write anything.
40. **Simon Peyton Jones, "How to Write a Great Research Paper"** (talk, free).
41. **Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style*** (2014).

## Suggested order

| Months | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tier 0 (Mahajan #1) + one Fermi problem daily. Hamming (#38) and McEnerney (#39) in week one — both are one sitting. |
| 2–3 | Deutsch (#5) + Feynman (#6) + Ćirković (#25). Lem's *His Master's Voice* alongside. |
| 4 | Nagel (#12), McGinn (#13), Dennett (#17), Ed Yong (#14). |
| 5 | The Tier 4 paper stack (#27, #28) — now you can check their arithmetic yourself. |
| 6 | Gould vs Conway Morris (#23). Then rewrite the ant analogy and see what survives. |

**The point of month 6:** the analogy was worth having. Rewriting it after this list, and watching
which parts you no longer believe, is the actual exercise. Keep the old version — that's why it's
saved.
